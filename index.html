Core Framework
The Aperture Theory
Reality is not what happens to you. Reality is what you attend to, measure, and act on. Your "aperture" — what you notice, track, and optimize — determines what becomes real in your life.
the hypothesis
Aperture Hypothesis: The quality of your life is proportional to the quality of what you choose to measure and track. What you don't measure doesn't improve. What you measure compounds. The AI layer makes continuous measurement possible at near-zero marginal cost.
experimental method
lifeOS treats your life as a series of hypotheses. Each habit, each goal, each focus area is a testable claim about what works. The AI tracks outcomes, identifies patterns, and suggests adjustments. You're not just logging — you're running experiments.
the three layers
1. Observe: Track what actually happens (habits, energy, output)
2. Hypothesize: What would improve if you changed X?
3. Experiment: Run the change for 90 days, measure the result
visualization principle
Based on Tufte's data-ink ratio and Cleveland's hierarchy of perception: show the data, minimize decoration, use position over color. The timeline shows compounding. The habit grid shows consistency. The focus bar shows progress. Each visualization answers one question.

Habit Tracker
Small daily actions compound over decades. Track consistency, not perfection. 7-day streaks build momentum.
compound interest of habits
1% daily improvement = 37x improvement in one year. A 30-minute exercise habit done 6 days/week for 10 years = 2,600 hours of compound physical capital. The habit grid shows your consistency pattern — streaks matter more than intensity.
